The project

An instrument you play by moving.

Ground translates movement into musical arrangement. Inspired by the Chinese Five Elements and pentatonic music, it distributes five instrumental identities across a shared space. Participants can explore alone or together, discovering how location changes the balance between individual voices and blended textures.

01

Give each place a musical identity.

Every instrument has a focused main region and a broader auxiliary region. The center brings its character forward; the surrounding area introduces a softer texture. Overlapping regions combine instrumental voices, making movement a way of arranging the music.

02

Make transitions feel continuous.

A clear trigger is not enough: the system also needs to connect one moment to the next. Main phrases retain their timing, while auxiliary material fades between states. Volume, reverb, density, and attack distinguish the two layers. Three main clips per instrument add variation within a consistent identity.

03

Prototype the sound before the space.

The audio system is documented in Pure Data, with mouse mapping used to test position-driven behavior. Cubase sessions show how the instrumental material was prepared. The full installation concept proposes position tracking and projected visual feedback; the room renderings below are concept studies.
